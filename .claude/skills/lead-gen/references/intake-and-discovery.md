# Intake and Multichannel Discovery

## Intake

When a new business is introduced, collect or research:

- Business name and website.
- Product or service being sold.
- Geographic service area.
- Ideal customer, if already known.
- Main customer problem being solved.
- Pricing and business model, if available.
- Competitive advantages.
- Desired outcome: leads, sales, app downloads, appointments, partnerships or another measurable goal.
- Available marketing budget.
- Existing website analytics, CRM, ad accounts and authorized integrations, if any.

Research the business before asking questions its website or the supplied information already answers. Ask only essential follow-ups. Never invent product features, prices, testimonials or claims. If the user does not know the ideal customer, research and recommend the most promising segments.

Save the result to `leads/<slug>/profile.md` using `templates/business-profile.md`, marking each item as verified (with source), inferred, or unknown.

## Discovery channels

Choose channels to fit the business and audience; do not use the same strategy for every industry. Candidates:

- Instagram (always evaluated)
- LinkedIn
- Google Search and public business listings
- Public company websites and directories
- Relevant creator profiles and online communities
- Public industry announcements and business news
- Professional associations and organizations
- Authorized business databases and CRM integrations
- Other appropriate channels found during research

### Instagram

Treat Instagram as a dedicated discovery channel. Search for relevant public business profiles, creators, niche accounts, local businesses, communities and potential partners. Analyze public, permitted information:

- Bios and business descriptions
- Public posts and captions
- Relevant hashtags and topics
- Public business announcements
- Product or service offerings
- Public requests for recommendations or services
- Collaborations and audience alignment
- Other observable signals of a legitimate opportunity

State why each prospect could need the product. Use approved APIs, integrations and search tools where available. If a page cannot be accessed, say so and use alternative legitimate sources. Never claim access to private profiles, DMs, hidden engagement data or anything else you could not see.

### LinkedIn and B2B

For B2B offers, identify relevant companies, decision-makers, departments and roles. Look for documented needs: expansion, relevant hiring, new locations, service requests, procurement opportunities and other legitimate buying-intent signals. Use public business contact channels and permitted research methods. Never fabricate personal contact details.

### Local businesses

For location-dependent businesses, find relevant businesses and organizations in the service area. Prioritize by service fit, location, documented need and practical reachability.

### Consumers

For B2C offers, research audience segments, public communities, customer needs and permitted first-party audience data. A public post, like, follow or profile does not prove purchase intent.

## Buying-intent signals

Look for publicly documented or authorized signals, for example:

- Public announcement of expansion
- Hiring for a relevant capability
- Public request for recommendations
- Launch of a relevant new product
- Quote or booking request through the business's own systems
- Started but did not complete sign-up
- Voluntary subscription to relevant communications
- Engagement with an authorized marketing campaign

For every signal, record its **source, date, relevance and limitations**. A page view or content engagement alone never means someone is ready to buy. Never use private browsing histories, unauthorized cookies, private accounts or personal communications.
